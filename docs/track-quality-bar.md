# Track quality bar

The brief every track owner works to (written 13 Nov 2026 for the lead's track owners; the Muse fleet follows it when finishing handed-over track PRs). It sits on top of `docs/track-authoring.md`, which stays the contract.

Read this whole file and `CONTRIBUTING.md` (git, slot, and PR rules). Repo: OkGoDoIt/Billionaire-Kart, a static three.js kart racer (parody billionaire racers).

## What Roger wants, in his words
- "Pretty much all of the tracks need substantial improvements, but especially the new ones. Major quality improvements, just generally make them look a lot better and make them more fun and feel higher end and actually well designed. Please put more effort into the tracks."
- "We want to make sure track design turns out really, really good, as that's an important aspect of making the gameplay fun."
- "It should be professional and polished, like an actual commercial Nintendo Switch 2 game (not just some half-hearted tech demo). Keep it super high quality, polished, punchy, and very very fun!"

Earlier rounds failed this bar while passing every checklist (sim no FAIL, "7/10 frames have readable text"). Roger rejected Davos for "bare grey hills, identical sparse pines, a plain slab ramp, empty snowfields, flat light". Checklists are the floor, not the goal. The goal is a track a player would beg to race again.

## One owner per track at a time
Whoever holds a track owns its whole set of files: `src/tracks/<id>.js`, `src/models/props-<id>.js`, its thumbnail `assets/tracks/<id>.webp`, and its own entries in shared tables (`AD_SETS` in `src/render/textures.js`, its music block in `src/audio/audio.js`). Other agents may work on other tracks in parallel. If you truly need an engine change (a new road skin, material, obstacle type, camera option), keep it small, backward compatible, and isolated, list it at the top of the PR, and make sure every other track still renders the same. Do not touch menus, HUD, racers, or other tracks.

## The process (do all of it, per track)
1. **Play and diagnose first.** `node tools/track-map.mjs <id> <scratch>/map.png`, `node tools/sim.mjs --track <id> --races 6`, `node tools/track-shots.mjs <id> <scratch>/sheet.jpg`, and a full-lap race-camera sheet: `node tools/shot.mjs --url "?track=<id>&r=<racer>&auto=1&intro=0&seed=4" --at 3,6,9,...(every 3 s for one lap) --out <scratch>/race.png --info`, plus the intro (`intro` omitted) and one 844x390 phone frame (`--size 844x390 --mobile`). Racer ids: see `src/content/racers.js`. Look at every frame. Write a blunt critique (what is boring to drive, ugly, cheap-looking, confusing, empty, unreadable), as a player and as an art director.
2. **Plan by fun per effort.** Consider all of:
   - **Layout and driving:** corner rhythm and variety (drift chains, S-bends, a hairpin worth a purple mini-turbo), real elevation (climbs, crests you get air off, drops with a view), at least one genuine route choice (a split or earned shortcut with a real risk/reward, set up in `shortcuts` for rivals too), a crossover or overpass where it fits, jumps and trick ramps, boost pad and item row placement that creates decisions. Spline surgery is allowed and encouraged where the lap is dull; keep it inside `docs/track-authoring.md` (lap 950-1,400 m unless the track already differs by design, radius rules, 25 m separation except intended bridges). Roger's #135: "splits, height variation, crossovers — tracks feel boring."
   - **A signature moment** a player would describe to a friend, and landmarks at both ends of the lap at hero scale.
   - **Art direction:** one cohesive palette and mood per track; strong lighting (sun direction, warm/cool contrast, fog for depth, color grade via `look`/`skyOverrides`); good materials (no flat untextured slabs where a texture or detail would read); three depth layers in every frame (trackside, mid-distance structures, a real horizon/backdrop); dense, intentional trackside dressing that frames the road (fences, posts, crowds, props, foliage with variety in size and type); motion (things that move, animate, blink, fly, smoke).
   - **Humor:** the billionaire-parody jokes should be big, visual, and readable from the kart at speed: set pieces at the road at hero scale, short punchy sign copy (a touch of wit, never generic), a joke every few seconds without turning the track into a billboard alley. For each main joke, name the real reference and what the scene makes fun of. On the race-camera approach, check whether someone can explain the joke after seeing it. Recognizing the individual props is only the first step.
   - **Lap-by-lap change (#121):** if your track has none, add one players notice from the kart, ideally one that changes the driving (an opened shortcut, a new hazard, a raised bridge, a draining lake), not just a tint.
   - **Missing set pieces (#87):** check `docs/track-design-<theme>.md` for promised set pieces your track lacks.
   - **Polish:** no floating props, z-fighting, clipping through the road, popping, visible seams, signs facing the wrong way or mirrored, unreadable text, blank backs, edges of the world, fog walls hiding nothing. Item boxes, coins, and boost pads placed with intent.
3. **Build in rounds.** After each round, re-shoot the same frames and compare side by side. Expect at least two rounds. Keep the game bootable at every commit.
4. **Verify (all must pass):** zero page/console errors; `node tools/sim.mjs --track <id> --races 6` no FAIL, rescues under 1 per race, hits under ~32, lap avg sane; if you added shortcuts, `--level hard` shows rivals using them without more rescues; draw calls under 800 and triangles under 1.8M at the worst race-camera frame (`--info`), and note phone-frame cost; `node tools/text-check.mjs --track <id>` clean; `node tools/sim.mjs --quick`, `node tools/bot.mjs`, and `node tools/bot.mjs --mobile` (all print ok).
5. **PR per track** (branch `track/<id>-quality`), title "<Track name>: quality pass", body with: the blunt critique, what changed and why, before/after race-camera sheets at the same timestamps and seed, the intro, the phone frame, map before/after if the layout changed, sim and draw-call numbers before/after, and an honest "Not verified / still weak" section. Regenerate the thumbnail from the new intro (`tools/track-thumbs.mjs`, or encode the verified intro frame to 640x360 webp). Comment on the related issues (#135, #121, #87, and the track's own issue) with the PR link. Do not merge; the lead reviews every frame.

## Resources and limits
- Heavy jobs: at most two on the whole machine (browser/sim/bot), enforced by `tools/slot.mjs`; several agents share them, so waits are normal. Batch your captures (one `shot.mjs` call with many `--at` times beats many calls). Never run tools in parallel or kill other processes.
- Shipped tracks that are currently the strongest reference points in this repo: Mars Colony, Crypto Cove, Silicon Valley's Golden Gate crossing. Learn how they build things (instancing, merged geometry, `fitText` signs, atlases) before writing your own.
- Image generation exists (`tools/gen-image.mjs`, key in `.env` as OPENAI_KEY, model id `gpt-image-2.6-sunburst`) for a painted horizon/backdrop or sign art if it raises quality; keep generated assets reproducible (prompt in the tool script) and parody-safe.
- Time: this is the most important work in the project right now. Spend the effort.

## Finishing a handed-over track PR (Muse fleet)
Several track PRs were left as drafts titled "WIP — handover to the Muse fleet" with a numbered checklist in the PR body. To finish one:
1. Claim it with a comment on the PR, then check out that PR's branch and merge `origin/main` into it (never start a new branch for it, never force-push).
2. Do the checklist **in order, one item per commit**, exactly as specified. Do not redesign the track or skip ahead; if a step is impossible as written, stop and comment on the PR with what you found instead of improvising.
3. After each item, shoot the frames that item names, look at them, and post them in a PR comment with one line on what changed.
4. Keep everything in this document's Verify section passing. When the checklist is done, update the PR body, mark it ready for review, and comment on the track's issue. Do not merge; the lead reviews.


## Generated art (Roger, 13 Nov: "use GPT image ... to make graphic assets, that might help us make this look better")
Use it wherever art beats procedural canvas drawing. The key lives in the repo's gitignored `.env` as `OPENAI_KEY`; in a separate worktree, symlink that `.env` in. Never print or commit it. Tool: `node tools/gen-image.mjs --out <png> --prompt "..." [--size 1536x1024] [--quality high] [--background transparent] [--ref a.png]`, default model `gpt-image-2.6-sunburst`.
Good uses: a painted 360 horizon panorama per track (the engine already supports `def.backdrop` with `assets/backdrops/<id>.webp`; the Cup 3 tracks have none yet), billboard and sign art, hull/building decals and logos (invented parody brands only), tileable surface textures (rack fronts, deck teak, snow, carpet), skyline cutouts with transparent backgrounds for mid-distance layers. Rules: one consistent art style per track (stylized, bright, Nintendo-like, not photoreal), match the existing look; convert to webp at the size actually needed (e.g. `magick in.png -resize 2048x -quality 82 out.webp`) and keep each under ~400 KB; load as image textures, not canvases (Safari caps canvas memory, see PR #211); keep generation reproducible by saving each prompt in a small script or a `prompts.md` next to the assets under `tools/` or `assets/<area>/`; look at every generated image before using it and regenerate anything off-model, with garbled text, or with real logos.
